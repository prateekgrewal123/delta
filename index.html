FLIGHT PRICING GUIDE

How flight prices are determined

Ever notice how flight prices seem to fluctuate? Airfare can change based on demand, timing, route, airline, season and other factors. Understanding these factors can help you plan smarter and compare options more effectively.

#1  Timing Is Key

Booking too early or too late can affect the fare you see. Prices can move as seats sell, so checking different travel dates and comparing available options can help you find a suitable fare.

#2  High and Low Seasons

Travel demand often rises during holidays, summer periods and major events. Off-peak travel can sometimes provide more affordable options, depending on the destination and availability.

#3  Time of Day Matters

Flight prices can vary by departure time. Early-morning, late-evening and overnight options may have different pricing from popular midday departures.

#4  Best Days to Travel

Midweek travel can sometimes offer different fares from busy Friday and weekend departures. Compare several nearby dates instead of checking only one day.

#5  Destination Popularity

Popular routes have strong demand, while less-served destinations may have fewer choices. Competition between airlines can also influence the fares available on a route.

#6  Airline Choice

Low-cost and full-service airlines can have different fare structures. Compare the total trip value, including baggage and other optional fees, before choosing an option.

#7  Fuel Prices and Taxes

Fuel costs, airport charges, government taxes and other surcharges can influence airline pricing. The final amount can therefore vary between routes and providers.

#8  Direct vs. Connecting Flights

Connecting flights may sometimes be less expensive than nonstop options. Compare total travel time, number of stops and the final fare before booking.
